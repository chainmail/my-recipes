This pizza base can be used for stuffed crust and normal pizza bases. It's a big job, so do it a day in advance, or reckon on a busy afternoon.

500g flour
2 tsp salt
1 sachet dried yeast
300ml water
3 tbsp olive oil (don't worry about this. Much will end up in the bowl after the first rise)
2 tsp sugar

First steps:
Mix water, yeast and sugar and wait for the yeast to activate (go frothy).
Put flour, salt into stand mixer.
Add yeast mix and ~1 tbsp olive oil.
Mix on low until the dough is shaggy & sticks to the sides.
Put the olive oil in a bowl, and brush it up the sides of the bowl.
Transfer the dough to the bowl with a spatula. It will be too sticky to use your hands.
Roll the dough lump around in the oil and push it down flat.
Any oil pooled around the edges should be smoothed over the surface of the dough.
Cover with damp tea towel and leave for about 90 minutes, or in the fridge overnight until at least doubled in volume.
If you use the fridge, take it out 30 minutes before you want to proceed so temperatures can normalise.

When risen:
Check the dough for stickiness. If it is too sticky to handle easily, pour some flour on to the workspace.
Pull out the dough for the number of pizza bases you want to make. There should be enough dough for about 6.
For each lump of dough, roll it in to a ball in your hands, working in extra flour to mitigate the stickiness.
Place each dough lump on to baking paper sheet and let it spread out and leave for about another 30 minutes.

After second rise:
Repeat the exercise of rolling the dough in to a ball, and adding more flour if necessary.
Place each piece in to the center of a baking paper sheet and spread it out, aiming for a circle.
Try to ensure some depth (~2 or 3 mm) evenly.
At this point, you can add the crust filling around the edge of the disk. Fold the edge over the filling and seal it to the base. Try to avoid any holes.
Bake the base at 180C for 5-10 minutes. This will fix the base in time and shape. It can now be left for a day or so, ideally in the fridge.

Left over dough should be made in to garlic butter knots. The remaining dough can be put in the fridge overnight, or just left on the side for an hour or so.
