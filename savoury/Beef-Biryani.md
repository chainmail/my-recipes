Serves about 6

Main ingredients:

    500g Beef mince
    300-400g beef
    4 large onions, thinly sliced or a couple handfuls of shop-bought fried onions
    4 large tomatoes, thinly sliced
    3 or more green bird’s eye chillies, left whole.
    3 tbsp full-fat yogurt
    1 tablespoon coriander powder
    1½ teaspoons red chilli powder
    ½ teaspoon turmeric
    ½ teaspoon paprika
    ¼ teaspoon ground black pepper
    2 tablespoons ginger garlic paste
    1 large handful fresh coriander (cilantro), roughly chopped
    1 large handful mint leaves, roughly chopped
    2 tablespoons lemon juice
    0.5 tsp orange food colouring powder mixed with about 4 tbsp water (optional)
    3 tbsp kewra water (optional)
    ~1l water

Whole spices:

    5 green cardamom pods, lightly split.
    3 black cardamom pods, lightly split.
    4 cloves
    2 1-inch pieces cinnamon
    1 bay leaf
    1.5 tbsp cumin seeds
    20 curry leaves

For the rice:

    1 kg long grain premium quality Basmati rice
    0.5 tbsp salt
    4 green cardamom pods, lightly split.

In advance:

Preheat the oven to 180C fan. Line a large baking tray with baking paper. Spread the mince out on the lined baking tray and cook in the oven for 40 minutes, breaking it up well with a wooden spoon every 10 minutes. It should have a dark, even colour and resemble large coffee granules. Remove from the oven and set aside.

Mix the following together in a bowl: yogurt, coriander powder, red chilli powder, turmeric, paprika, black pepper, salt.

4 tbsp water and food colouring.

Pour oil into a large sauce pan over a medium-high heat. When it is visibly hot, add the sliced onions and salt and stir well. Continue frying for about 15 to 20 minutes or until the onions are beginning to turn a deep golden brown. Transfer the fried onions to a plate.

Rinse the rice. Strain and then cover with fresh water to soak for at least 30 minutes.
Pre cook the rice with cardamoms to about 50% done.

For the Biryani masala:

    1. Pour oil into a large saucepan over a medium-high heat and add back half of the onions.
    2. Add ginger garlic paste and beef, and sauté until the beef has a good sear on it, about 3 to 5 minutes.
    3. Add the whole spices and stir until aromatic.
    4. Add the ground spice/yoghurt mix and stir to combine.
    5. Add 300ml stock.
    6. Reduce the heat and add tomatoes, green chillies and half the prepared onions.
        - Cover and cook until the beef is tender and the onions and tomatoes have cooked down into a thick paste.
        - If necessary, add splashes of water to help tenderise the beef and prevent burning at the bottom of the pot.
    7. Gently stir in the precooked mince.
    8. Cook until the oil begins to separate from the gravy.
    9. Remove from the heat and mix in the lemon juice, half of each of the coriander/mint and set aside.

Assembly:

    In a large casserole, spread half the beef masala.
    Then spread out half of the boiled rice, followed by the rest of the masala.
    Cover with the remaining rice.
    Drizzle the food colour mix, if desired, but do not overdo it.
    Add the remaining coriander and mint.
    Sprinkle over the prepared fried onions.
    Cover with the lid.
    Cook in the oven at 130C until the rice has finished cooking, about 90 minutes.
    Drizzle over the kewra water.
